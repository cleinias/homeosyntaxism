## Step (b) — Patterns

Each pattern operationalizes one or more claims as a formal feature. Be concrete about the linguistic signature; state the form's intended effect; predict what would happen in three scenarios — `autonomous` (form transfers cleanly to any domain), `with_content` (form interacts with domain to produce a related but modified effect), `hollow` (the effect doesn't survive content substitution). Tag scope and generation task.

### Pattern P_1

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | *sarcastic antiphrastic descriptions* |
| Form (with linguistic signature) | Narrative descriptions use words or phrases that mean the exact opposite of their literal definition. In the Spanish-garrison passage of the incipit: "insegnavan la modestia alle fanciulle e alle donne del paese" (=sexually assault), "accarezzavan di tempo in tempo le spalle a qualche marito, a qualche padre" (= bastonare), "diradar l'uve, e alleggerire a' contadini le fatiche della vendemmia" (= stealing the harvest). Signature: benevolent/pedagogical/charitable verbs predicated of coercive or predatory actions, with no narratorial correction. |
| Effect claim | Allows the author to criticize the described actions without appearing to pass judgments; the antiphrastic clauses form a micro-ensemble that anticipates the logic of power and points to a social universe divided into oppressed and oppressors (A11) |
| Mapping prediction — autonomous | The inversion mechanism transfers intact: target-domain prose applies tender, approving, or honorific vocabulary to actions the context marks as violent, coercive, or blameworthy, and the reader reconstructs the condemnation from the gap between word and deed. The critique arrives without a single evaluative statement from the narrator — the same deniable sarcasm, new referents. |
| Mapping prediction — with-content | The form collides with the target domain's own euphemistic conventions (bureaucratic, corporate, political registers already say "accarezzare" for "bastonare" in earnest). The authorial antiphrasis becomes indistinguishable from the domain's institutional doublespeak, and the effect shifts from the narrator's sarcasm to an exposure of the domain's language itself — the irony reads as quotation of how this world talks, a second-order critique the source effect didn't have.|
| Mapping prediction — hollow | The inversion is formally present (a lexeme meaning the opposite of the denoted action, verifiable against context) but the reader has no shared frame that makes the literal reading impossible: the tender phrasing reads as plain description or odd affection, not irony. The criticism vanishes; the sentence seems merely imprecise. Constraint satisfied on the page, effect dead — antiphrasis degraded to misnaming.|

### Pattern P_2

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | *metric verses are interspersed in the prose* |
| Form (with linguistic signature) | The prose inserts a few prosodic structures, like 'novenari', 'endecasillabi', 'settenari' into the narrative descriptions. | Effect claim | The prose embeds canonical Italian metric feet (novenari, endecasillabi, settenari) inside descriptive sentences. The form does its rhythmic work below the lexical level; semantic content is interchangeable. |
| Mapping prediction — with-content | The metric substrate still paces the prose, but in a target domain whose conventional register is non-literary (bureaucratic, technical, journalistic), the embedded verses surface as audible ornament rather than dissolving into the flow. The pacing function survives but acquires a second-order effect — mock-elevation, parodic nobility, or ironized lyricism — because hendecasyllables in administrative description foreground themselves |

### Pattern P_7

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | *lucid sinuosity ("lucido serpeggiamento" as stylistic invariant)* |
| Form (with linguistic signature) | Extended periods whose syntactic shape mimics the meandering of the referent while never losing transparency: high subordinate/coordinate depth, chained appositions and relative clauses that wind like the water ("di là braccio di fiume, poi lago, poi fiume ancora, che va a perdersi in lucido serpeggiamento pur tra' monti che l'accompagnano, degradando via via, e perdendosi quasi anch'essi nell'orizzonte"), yet with fully legible hierarchy — no anacoluthon, exact lexicon, the reader never loses the thread. | Mapping prediction — hollow | The sentences wind (depth and chaining verifiable) but either lucidity collapses under the subordination or no phrase in the text can be read as its own formula — syntax passes the audit while the aderenza, the deepest part of the claim, fails. |

### Pattern P_8

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | *historicized-present puncture (tense/deixis friction)* |
| Form (with linguistic signature) | A naturalistic present-tense description is punctured by a temporal deixis that anchors the describing voice in its own historical present: "un gran borgo al giorno d'oggi, e che s'incammina a diventar città". Signature: (i) surrounding description in the atemporal/naturalistic present; (ii) a now-adverb indexing the narrator's moment ("al giorno d'oggi"); (iii) a present-tense verb that the adverb historicizes ("s'incammina" — ongoing change, dated from the narrator's vantage). The result is Romagnoli's "stacco" between the *presente naturalistico* of the landscape and the *presente storicizzato* of the narrator. |
| Effect claim | The puncture makes the geographical space historical: the *stacco* between the description's naturalistic present and the narrator's historicized present introduces time into space, binding the writing to a reality that is at once geographical and historical — the Lecco sentence is an "attualizzatore dello spazio" (A6), the next in the series that "Quel" opened (A3). The described place stops being a timeless backdrop and acquires a present that implies a past and a future. |
| Mapping prediction — autonomous | Any atemporal descriptive register punctured by a now-deixis temporalizes the described space the same way: the place stops being a timeless backdrop and acquires a present that implies a past and a future. The stacco does its work regardless of what is described — the puncture installs a dated vantage inside the description, and through it time enters the space. |
| Mapping prediction — with-content | In a domain with its own historicity (institutions that reform, technologies that obsolesce, settlements that grow), the puncture binds the description to the domain's real history: the "al giorno d'oggi" marks the described state as one moment in a datable trajectory, and the stacco acquires domain content — progress, decay, supersession. The actualizing function is amplified: the description is tied not just to a real place but to that place's ongoing time. |
| Mapping prediction — hollow | The deixis is formally present but no stacco registers: either the surrounding prose never establishes the naturalistic present the adverb must rupture, or the domain's register is already routinely time-indexed (journalistic, bureaucratic prose timestamps everything), so "al giorno d'oggi" reads as ordinary time reference. The space stays a timeless backdrop — no time enters it. Constraint verifiable, friction dead. |

## At the end

You should have:

- An (a) table with N claims, grouped into ~5–10 clusters depending on the article.
- A (b) table with M patterns covering all scopes and generation tasks present in the reading. Most patterns are clause-level / prose; macro-architectural patterns are rarer but methodologically important.
- A (c) table with K examples — typically the same order of magnitude as M, though one example can instantiate several patterns and one pattern can be instantiated by several examples.
- A (d) section with K Evidence Records — one per example.

When you hand this off to Phase 2, the **patterns table (b)** travels forward as the `patterns.json` spec, and the **Evidence Records (d)** travel as documentation that anchors the Phase 4 audit. The claims (a) and examples (c) stay with the Phase 1 artifact for traceability.

If something doesn't fit the rubric, write a note in the relevant cell or block prefixed with `[unresolved:]` and continue. See "What to do if you get stuck" in `Phase_1_Critic_Rubric.md`.
